# Insight

#manual#

After you read, think and write for a while, the workspace knows things about you that you have not noticed. The Insight views show them.

## Graph

`Ctrl/Cmd + Shift + G`, or the graph icon of the side menu.

The Graph draws your workspace as a network:

- **Notes** are the round nodes, linked by `[[wiki links]]`. A note with many links is drawn bigger.
- **Books** are small upright books (with a spine), joined to their annotation note and to every note that links to them with `{{ }}`. The line between a book and its annotations is solid and thick.
- **Issues** are rings like those of GitHub: green for open, grey for closed, linked to the notes that mention them and the notes they point to.
- **Hashtags** tie together the notes that share them.

Drag a node to pull the network around, scroll to zoom, drag the background to move, click a node to open it. Isolated notes drift away from the cluster: a good way to find the ideas that have not been connected yet.

> Reading a book alone gives you a node. Reading it with highlights, linking the highlights to notes and issues, gives you a constellation.

## Chronicle

`Ctrl/Cmd + Shift + C`, or the calendar icon.

- **Journals**: one note a day. *Today* opens (or creates) `writing/daily/<date>.md`. All daily notes are listed under **Journals**.
- **Activity heat map**: a GitHub-style calendar of how much you did each day. Writing counts edits to notes. When you have read books, a switch shows **All**, **Writing** or **Reading**, and a hover shows both numbers. Reading is counted in 15 minute steps of the time the Reader measured.
- **Timeline**: below the heat map, notes are listed by the day they were last changed. Click a cell of the heat map to jump to that day.

The Chronicle answers "what did I do last Tuesday?" and "have I been neglecting my reading?" without any bookkeeping on your side.

## Hashtags

The **Hashtag** tab of the sidebar is a map of your vocabulary of topics. The more you tag, the more it tells you about where your attention goes.

## Backlinks and mentions

At the foot of every note: the notes that link to it, and the notes that mention its title without a link. Good for finding the hidden structure of a subject. See [[4 Linking|Linking]].
